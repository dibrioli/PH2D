**The example the user points at may be the EXCEPTION of its family.** Enio asked why the
MiniCavalry oscillator has three output pins and ours has one (2026-09-04). Generalizing from
that node ("he bundles side-outputs, we don't") would have been wrong: a census of ALL 30 of
his multi-output nodes showed **47 of 49** extra pins are byproducts/decompositions (legitimate
under the same law our 4 of 4 obey), and the **only 2 recomputations in his catalogue were on
the oscillator** — the node he pointed at.

**Why:** a user points at the case that struck them, not at a representative one. A design
verdict derived from one node inherits whatever is peculiar about that node. Doc 100 (Motion
Nodes) reached the opposite conclusion from the one the example suggested — *don't copy the
pins; the tee is bit-identical and stays on the device* — only because the family was counted
first.

**How to apply:** when a comparison starts from one example, (1) enumerate the family in the
SOURCE (grep/parse, not memory), (2) classify every member by the MECHANISM that produces it
(byproduct · decomposition · projection · recomputation …), (3) locate the example in that
table before writing a verdict. Related: [[feedback_a_measured_refusal_answers_one_question_recheck_it_when_yours_is_another]],
